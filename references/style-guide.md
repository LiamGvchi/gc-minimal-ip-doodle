# Fixed Visual Grammar

Use this document to keep the visual style stable across different IP characters. Species-specific anatomy belongs to the identity lock, not to the style.

## Composition

- Use a square canvas unless the user specifies another format.
- Use the selected background mode: pure or near-white, or one pale contextual color with controlled micro-grain.
- Keep one character or one compact character-and-prop cluster near the visual center.
- Keep the subject deliberately small, usually around one-third to one-half of the canvas width and height.
- Preserve broad, quiet margins on every side.
- Avoid scenery, horizon lines, rooms, decorative frames, and background furniture.
- A short ground line, curb, stool, mat, or one supporting object is acceptable when the action needs it.

## Line

- Use thick black or near-black outlines with rounded joins.
- Keep line weight visually consistent across character and props.
- Allow mild wobble, asymmetry, and imperfect curves so the result feels hand drawn.
- Use only a few interior lines for limbs, facial features, folds, fins, or object details.
- Avoid mechanically perfect vector curves, hairline detail, dense hatching, and logo-like geometry.

## Color

- Preserve the character's signature palette from the reference.
- Use flat, opaque color blocks with restrained secondary colors.
- Make each enclosed color region read as one uniform fill with no internal tonal variation.
- Keep `white` backgrounds uncolored. For `soft-grain`, use one high-lightness, low-saturation hue chosen to separate the character from the canvas.
- Use one small accent color when it belongs to the character or prop.
- Avoid gradients, glossy highlights, realistic reflected light, atmospheric tint, texture overlays, and volumetric shading.

## Background Modes

### White

- Use pure or near-white across the full canvas.
- Add no grain, paper texture, shadow, vignette, or atmospheric tint.
- Use this mode by default when the user does not mention a background.

### Soft Grain

- Select the background only after inspecting the accepted character image and its dominant colors.
- Use a pale, low-saturation hue that supports the scene mood while staying distinct from skin, white clothing, cream fur, and signature color blocks.
- Keep the background visually close to off-white; it must not become a dark, saturated, or scene-like color field.
- Add extremely fine, even, low-contrast monochrome grain at roughly 20-25% of a visibly grainy reference. Keep most grain in the background and only a barely perceptible amount inside character fills.
- Keep white areas readable and every black outline crisp, solid, and visually dominant.
- Apply this mode by editing the accepted white master so character identity and composition do not drift.

## Character Simplification

- Favor a rounded silhouette, compressed anatomy, and readable front or three-quarter poses.
- Simplify limbs and details without deleting identity-defining anatomy.
- Use dot eyes, simple circles, or half-lidded eyes according to the source character.
- Use a tiny mouth and very few expression marks.
- Preserve asymmetry or unusual features when they identify the character.

## Mood

- Aim for quiet cute, sleepy, blank, friendly, mildly awkward, or deadpan.
- Let humor come from the mismatch between a simple action and a low-energy expression.
- Use at most a few motion, heat, rain, or surprise lines.
- Avoid dramatic action, exaggerated screaming, cinematic emotion, or crowded slapstick.

## Prop Density

- Prefer one primary prop and zero to two tiny supporting marks or objects.
- Make props subordinate to the character.
- Keep object shapes simple enough to read at sticker size.
- Do not build a full environment around the prop.

## Hard Avoids

- Photorealism or realistic animal anatomy.
- 3D toy, clay, plush, fur, plastic, rubber, or collectible rendering.
- Realistic shadows, cast shadows, rim light, depth of field, or cinematic lighting.
- Heavy grain, coarse noise, dirty paper stains, colored speckles, airbrush volume, painterly blending, or heavy antialias polish. Controlled micro-grain is allowed only in `soft-grain` mode.
- Complex backgrounds, multiple unrelated characters, or scene-filling furniture.
- Text, captions, labels, speech bubbles, logos, and watermarks unless explicitly requested.
- Exact copying of a bundled reference image's pose, action, or composition.
